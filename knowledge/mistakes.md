## [ER-001] Crashing into a body
tags: crash, collision, planet, moon, surface, periapsis, orange marker
level: beginner

A crash happens when the probe comes closer to a body's center than its radius. The preview shows it before you launch: an orange line ending in a ring. Common causes are a launch power that is too low, so the probe falls back onto its own planet, or a close pass whose closest point is below the target's surface. To fix it, raise the closest point of the path with a prograde burn on the far side, or change the aim so the path passes farther from the body.

Try: look at the orange ring and move the aim until the line misses the body.

## [ER-002] Leaving the map
tags: lost, map, bounds, escape, too fast, overshoot, power
level: beginner

If the probe travels beyond the map bounds, the attempt ends with a message saying how long it took. This usually means the launch power was too high or an assist threw the probe outward. If your launch power is above the escape speed of the planet you left, the probe will not fall back. Lower the power a little, or add a retrograde burn to slow down. The preview turns orange and the message line says the launch leaves the map before you fly.

Try: reduce power by about ten percent and check the preview again.

## [ER-003] Flying past the target
tags: too fast, flyby, overshoot, speed, miss, past
level: beginner

If the probe passes the target and keeps going, it arrived faster than the target's escape speed. Gravity bent the path but could not hold it. Arrive more slowly by launching with a bit less power, or burn retrograde near the closest approach. Aiming a little ahead of a moving target helps too, because it will have moved by the time you arrive. A close pass that hugs the surface bends the path more than a distant one but risks a crash.

Try: lower the launch power slightly, or add a small retrograde node near the target.

## [ER-004] Cannot hold the orbit band
tags: band, hold, orbit, timer, reset, ellipse, circular
level: intermediate

The hold timer restarts whenever the probe leaves the radius band, so an orbit that swings out of the band each lap never finishes. This happens when the orbit is too elongated, with a periapsis below the inner radius or an apoapsis beyond the outer one. Aim for a near-circular orbit at the middle of the band. Adjust it with a burn at the periapsis or apoapsis: a prograde burn raises the opposite side and a retrograde burn lowers it.

Try: compare the preview's periapsis and apoapsis with the band edges, then burn to bring them closer together.
