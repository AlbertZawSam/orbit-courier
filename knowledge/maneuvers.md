## [MA-001] What a burn node is
tags: burn node, node, maneuver, add, drag, handle, dv
level: beginner

A burn node is an instant change in velocity at a chosen time after launch. Click the preview line, or press N, to add one. Select it to show two handles: brass for prograde and teal for radial. Drag a handle to set the delta-v, or use plus and minus for prograde and comma and period for radial. Each node costs fuel equal to its delta-v and levels limit how many nodes you can place. The preview updates live, and you can edit future nodes during flight.

Try: add a node, drag one handle a little, and read the change in the preview.

## [MA-002] A burn at apoapsis raises the periapsis
tags: apoapsis, periapsis, prograde, raise, circularize, hohmann, orbit
level: intermediate

Speeding up at one point of an orbit raises the opposite side of it. A prograde burn at the apoapsis lifts the periapsis, which is the standard way to turn a stretched ellipse into a rounder orbit, and a prograde burn at periapsis raises the apoapsis. Burning where the probe is slowest and farthest is the most efficient place to change the periapsis. A transfer between two circular orbits uses one burn to leave the first and a second to settle into the next.

Try: if your path dips into a planet, add a small prograde node on the far side of the loop.

## [MA-003] Slowing down to be captured
tags: retrograde, capture, slow, brake, closest approach, flyby
levels: L3
level: intermediate

Arriving fast at a moon means moving above its escape speed relative to it, so gravity bends the path but does not hold you. A retrograde burn near the closest approach lowers your speed relative to the moon, which makes the orbit bound. Even a small burn can be enough, and timing is forgiving because anywhere near the closest approach works. In level 3 a retrograde burn of about 0.3 around 5.6 seconds after launch works, and any time from about 4.8 to 6.4 seconds does too.

Try: click the path near where it passes the moon and drag the brass handle backward.

## [MA-004] Slow motion planning
tags: slow motion, slow-mo, time, budget, planning, S key
level: beginner

Press S during flight to slow time to one tenth speed. The budget is measured in real seconds and is shown in the HUD, so use it where precision matters, such as dragging a node's handle near a close pass. Slow motion does not change the physics, only how fast you watch it, so the flight and the preview stay identical. When the budget runs out, time returns to normal automatically.

Try: save slow motion for placing a burn just before a close approach.

## [MA-005] Radial burns
tags: radial, burn, correction, steer, sideways
level: intermediate

A radial burn is perpendicular to your motion, so it turns your velocity rather than speeding it up or slowing it down. That makes it useful for small course corrections, for example to shift a close pass a little to one side. It costs fuel in the same way as any burn, equal to its delta-v. To change the size of an orbit, prograde and retrograde burns are more direct, and combining both lets you aim and adjust speed together.

Try: if the path misses the target by a little, try a small radial burn early in the flight.
