## [HZ-001] Asteroid fields
tags: asteroid, asteroids, rocks, field, ring, hazard, collision
level: intermediate

An asteroid field is a ring of small rocks orbiting a body. The rocks follow fixed orbits, so they move the same way every attempt, and they do not pull on the probe, but touching one is a crash. The preview shows your path crossing the field, and a crash marker if it hits a rock. Passing outside the ring or inside its inner edge is safe, and crossing quickly at a steady course gives the rocks less time to line up with you. The timing of your arrival matters because the rocks keep moving.

Try: check the preview for an orange marker inside the ring and shift your launch time or angle until it clears.

## [HZ-002] Solar wind
tags: solar wind, wind, push, zone, acceleration, drift, hazard
level: intermediate

A solar wind zone pushes the probe with a steady force while it is inside, and the direction of the push turns slowly over time. The push fades smoothly near the edge of the zone, so there is no sudden jolt. The preview already includes the wind, so the line you see is the path you will fly. You can use the push on purpose: aim so that it carries you onto the target, or burn against it. The longer you stay inside the zone, the more it moves you.

Try: watch how the preview bends inside the zone and aim off to one side so the wind carries you back.

## [HZ-003] Dark mass
tags: dark mass, dark, strong pull, black, assist, flyby, fly past, close pass, closest approach, danger, hazard
level: advanced

A dark mass is a tiny body with an enormous pull. Flying past it can fling the probe a long way with no fuel, which makes it a strong gravity assist, but a path that comes too close is a crash because its surface is very small. Near it the probe moves very fast, tens of units per second, so keep your closest approach comfortably wide: the farther the pass, the gentler and more predictable the bend. Use the preview to check the closest-approach pin before you commit.

Try: increase the miss distance a little at a time and watch the bend and the closest-approach label.
